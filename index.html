Core Pronunciation Concepts

1. Voiced vs. Unvoiced Sounds

Throat Test

Put your fingers gently on your throat! Some consonants make your vocal cords vibrate (buzz) — these are voiced. Others use just air with no vibration — these are unvoiced.

Voiced (Vibration!)
/b/ /d/ /g/ /z/ /v/ /m/ /n/ /ŋ/ /l/ /ð/ /ʒ/ /dʒ/ /w/ /r/ /j/
Unvoiced (Just Air)
/p/ /t/ /k/ /s/ /f/ /θ/ /ʃ/ /tʃ/

Tip: All English vowels are voiced!

2. Long vs. Short Vowels

Tip Box :

When you see two dots : after a vowel symbol, it means it is a l-o-n-g sound! Changing length changes the word meaning:

/i:/ sheep (long) vs. /ɪ/ ship (short)
/ɔ:/ ball (long) vs. /ɒ/ clock (short)
/u:/ moon (long) vs. /ʊ/ book (short)
Other long vowels in our lessons: /ɑ:/ heart, car • /ɜ:/ girl, bird.

3. Diphthongs (Gliding Vowels)

2-in-1 Glide

A diphthong starts in one vowel position and smoothly glides to a second position inside a single syllable. Feel your tongue and lips move!

4. The Schwa /ə/ & Weak vs. Strong Forms

Lesson 19

/ə/ is the most common English vowel. It is never stressed — always short, relaxed, and weak: banana, pepper, mother, a glass of water.

Weak form: "Can you sing?" → /kən/
Strong form (at end): "Yes, I can." → /kæn/

5. Linking /s/ Across Words

Lesson 20

When one word ends in /s/ or /z/ and the next starts with /s/, we connect them smoothly into one long /s/ instead of pausing:

this_seat this_suit Sam's_seat Emma's_sewing
